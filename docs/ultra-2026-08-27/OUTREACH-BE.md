## 1. The official line (one paragraph)

Belgian law **forbids advertising by electronic mail without prior, free, specific and informed consent** of the addressee (**WER art. XII.13, § 1**). The King made two exceptions. The one that matters for first contact to a club is **KB 4 April 2003, art. 1, 2°**: the sender is exempt from that prior consent **when the addressee is a legal person (*rechtspersoon*) and the electronic contact details used for that purpose are impersonal**. A Belgian **vzw that has acquired legal personality** is a *rechtspersoon* (**WVV art. 2:6, § 2**). The *Verslag aan de Koning* and the **FOD Economie** spam FAQ treat **`info@…`, `contact@…`, `sales@…`** as the textbook impersonal addresses of a legal person. **GBA** treats a functional address used to reach a legal person as **in principle outside** the AVG direct-marketing definition — **unless** the facts let you tie that inbox to one identifiable natural person. **Opt-out remains mandatory on every advertising message**, including this exception (**WER art. XII.13, § 2** + **KB art. 2**). The exception does **not** cover a volunteer’s personal Hotmail, a `voornaam.naam@` mailbox, or a *feitelijke vereniging* with no legal personality.

That is the line. Everything below is how to stay on it, and where it stops.

## 2. Default rule — opt-in (WER XII.13)

**Source:** Wet van 15 december 2013 houdende invoeging van Boek XII in het Wetboek van economisch recht, *Belgisch Staatsblad* 14 januari 2014, numac **2013011667**. Justel: <https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/wet/2013/12/15/2013011667/justel>. Dutch text also on etaamb: <https://etaamb.openjustice.be/nl/wet-van-15-december-2013_n2013011667.html>. FOD Economie still quotes the same wording in *“Spam” in 23 vragen en antwoorden* (September 2019).

**Art. XII.13, § 1 (verified quote):**

> Het gebruik van elektronische post voor reclame is verboden zonder de voorafgaande, vrije, specifieke en geïnformeerde toestemming van de geadresseerde van de boodschappen.

**Art. XII.13, § 2 (verified):** on every advertising electronic-mail message the service provider must (1°) give clear information about the right to object to future advertising, and (2°) designate and make available a suitable electronic means to exercise that right.

**Art. XII.13, § 3 (verified) — forbidden methods:**

1. using a third party’s electronic address **or identity**;
2. falsifying or hiding information that lets the recipient recognise the **origin** of the message or the path it took;
3. encouraging the recipient to visit sites that breach art. XII.12.

**Art. XII.13, § 4 (verified):** the service provider must **prove** that advertising by electronic mail was requested. Burden of proof sits on the sender. For the legal-person exception, FOD FAQ Q13 says the sender must still be able to prove that the exemption applies.

**“Reclame” (WER art. I.18, 6°, verified in the same inserting law):** any communication intended to promote, directly or indirectly, goods, services, **or the image** of an undertaking, organisation or person exercising a commercial, industrial or craft activity or a regulated profession. A first-contact pitch that sells a club-site kit, a Peppol pack, or any other paid tool **is reclame**. Do not relabel it as “just saying hello” to dodge XII.13.

**Art. XII.12, 1°–2° (verified from the 2013 inserting law; Fiscosearch “meest recente versie” still matches):** advertising that is part of an information-society service must (1°) be immediately recognisable as advertising from the overall impression, including presentation; **“Indien dit niet het geval is”** it must carry, readable and unambiguous, the word **“reclame”**; and (2°) make the natural or legal person **for whose account** the advertising is done **clearly identifiable**. FOD FAQ Q15’s *quote* of XII.12, 1° drops the “Indien dit niet het geval is” clause; FOD’s *commentary* still says the word is **not** systematic and is required when the message is not otherwise recognisable. A cold pitch that looks like a personal note is the case for **Reclame** in the subject or opening. **UNVERIFIED** whether a one-to-one B2B mail to `info@` that is obviously a product offer still needs the word. Safer: make the commercial nature obvious, and identify the sender by real name + domain.

## 3. The exception you may use — impersonal address of a *rechtspersoon*

**Source:** Koninklijk besluit van 4 april 2003 tot reglementering van het verzenden van reclame per elektronische post, *Belgisch Staatsblad* 28 mei 2003, numac **2003011238**. Justel: <https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/besluit/2003/04/04/2003011238/justel>. etaamb (includes *Verslag aan de Koning*): <https://etaamb.openjustice.be/nl/koninklijk-besluit-van-04-april-2003_n2003011238>.

**KB art. 1, 2° (verified operative text):**

> In afwijking van artikel 14, § 1, eerste lid van de wet van 11 maart 2003 […] is elke dienstverlener ervan vrijgesteld de voorafgaande toestemming te vragen om reclame per elektronische post te ontvangen:  
> […]  
> **2° bij rechtspersonen als de elektronische contactgegevens die hij met dat doel gebruikt onpersoonlijk zijn.**

(The KB still cites the 11 March 2003 law; that rule now lives in **WER XII.13**. FOD FAQ 2019 applies the KB to XII.13.)

There is a **second** exception in KB art. 1, 1° (existing **customers**, similar products, opt-out offered at collection). That is **soft opt-in**. It is **not** the first-contact line. Do not pretend a club is already a customer.

### 3.1 A vzw can be a *rechtspersoon*

**Source:** Wetboek van vennootschappen en verenigingen (WVV), wet 23 maart 2019. Justel: <https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/wet/2019/03/23/2019A40586/justel>.

- **WVV art. 1:2:** a *vereniging* is formed by two or more members, pursues a disinterested purpose, and may not distribute assets to members. A **vzw** is that form **with** legal personality.
- **WVV art. 2:6, § 2:** **VZW’s verkrijgen rechtspersoonlijkheid vanaf de dag van de neerlegging** of the documents in art. 2:9, § 1, 1°, 3° and 4° (oprichtingsakte + extract + director appointments) at the registry of the enterprise court. FPS Justice citizen booklet *De vzw* states the same: the vzw has its own legal personality, separate from its members. PDF: <https://justitie.belgium.be/sites/default/files/ASBL-NL.pdf>.

**UNVERIFIED for any named club until you look it up:** that *this* mailbox belongs to a vzw that actually completed that filing. A **feitelijke vereniging** (no filing, no legal personality) is **not** a *rechtspersoon*. The KB art. 1, 2° exception **does not apply**. Do not invent a KBO number to paper over that. Look the name up in [KBO Public Search](https://kbopub.economie.fgov.be/kbopub/zoeknummerform.html) / [search by name](https://kbopub.economie.fgov.be/kbopub/zoeknaamform.html). If the entity is not there, do not mail under this exception.

### 3.2 `info@` is the impersonal address — named mailboxes are not

**Verslag aan de Koning (KB 4 April 2003, verified):** legal persons may hold addresses such as `info@…`, `contact@…`, `privacy@…`, `sales@…`, `bestelling@…`, `klantendienst@…`. Unsolicited advertising mail **may** be sent to those addresses **in so far as the circumstances make clear they are addresses of legal persons**.

**Same Verslag, opposite case (verified):** when the legal person assigns addresses tied to its domain of the form **`naam.voornaam@company.be`**, those are addresses of **natural persons**, whether used professionally or privately. Advertising to them **without prior consent of that natural person is not allowed**.

**FOD Economie FAQ Q13 (September 2019, verified from the official brochure):** repeats that *Verslag* almost verbatim. The exception applies **even if the legal person is not a customer**. The sender should check, including when buying or renting lists, and **must be able to prove** the exemption. Products or services advertised this way must **target legal persons**, not natural persons. The *Verslag* example of abuse: grocery / holiday / personal-clothing ads sent to a legal-person address to dodge opt-in. A **club website sold to the vzw** (the organisation buys a brochure site) is aimed at the legal person. A pitch aimed at **parents, members, or a treasurer as a consumer** is not. **UNVERIFIED** at the margin: a kit that also helps the club collect member payments — treat the mail as B2B to the vzw, and do not write as if you were selling to families.

**Do not mail:**

| Address shape | Line |
| --- | --- |
| `info@club.be`, `contact@club.be`, `secretariaat@club.be` on the club domain | Impersonal **if** circumstances show it is the vzw’s mailbox. This is the intended first-contact box. |
| `voornaam.naam@club.be`, `jan@club.be` | Natural person. Opt-in. **GBA** / AVG on top. |
| `secretaris@hotmail.com`, `voorzitter@gmail.com`, `…@outlook.com`, `…@skynet.be` | **Personal consumer inbox.** Not the exception. Do not mail. |
| `*@mail.twizzit.com` or a named volunteer’s Twizzit login mail **UNVERIFIED** | Not a generic `info@` on the club domain. Do not invent a rule; **do not mail** unless you have a cited public `info@` on the club’s own domain. |

**No personal Hotmail** is a **hard house rule** and it tracks the official split: Hotmail/Gmail/Outlook.com of a volunteer is a natural-person address. It is also a hard **from** rule (see §6).

## 4. Opt-out is mandatory — including on the exception

The legal-person exception removes **prior consent**. It does **not** remove the right of objection.

**WER XII.13, § 2** applies to **every** advertising electronic-mail message (FOD FAQ Q14, verified): even after consent, and **even in both KB exceptions**.

**KB art. 2 (verified):**

> Ieder persoon kan rechtstreeks aan een bepaalde dienstverlener zonder kosten en zonder een reden op te geven, zijn wil kenbaar maken om van hem geen reclame per elektronische post meer te ontvangen.

The service provider must:

1. within a **reasonable time**, send an electronic **acknowledgement** that the request is registered;
2. within a reasonable time, take the measures needed to honour that will;
3. **update lists** of persons who have objected.

The *Verslag aan de Koning* and FOD FAQ Q14 add (verified):

- that last acknowledgement **must not be promotional**;
- FOD reads “reasonable” as **never more than 24–48 hours** given that an auto-reply can be immediate;
- no fee, no reason demanded;
- after objection, **do not pass the address to third parties** even if the person had earlier agreed to sharing.

**House implementation (not a statutory formula):** every first-contact mail to `info@` includes a Dutch sentence the board can act on without a portal, for example: *Als u geen berichten meer van ons wilt, antwoord dan op deze mail met “stop”.* Honour **stop / uitschrijven / nee / verwijder** from that mailbox. Log it. Do not mail that domain again.

**Robinson list** (BAM, robinson.be) is **self-regulation** for members, mentioned in FOD FAQ Q14. It is **not** a substitute for the per-message opt-out in XII.13, § 2. **UNVERIFIED** whether a given sender is a BAM member. Do not skip the in-message opt-out because a Robinson check was done.

## 5. GBA — when `info@` is still personal data

**WER/KB** (spam / ePrivacy as implemented in Book XII) and **AVG/GBA** are two layers. FOD FAQ Q1 and Q7 say so. A functional `info@` can be **off the AVG** and still **on WER XII.13**.

**GBA Aanbeveling 01/2025** (*over de verwerking van persoonsgegevens bij direct marketing*), pts **24–27** (verified from the GBA PDF):

- Direct marketing in the GBA sense is communication to identified or identifiable **natural persons**.
- Sending a direct-marketing email to a **functional email address used to approach a legal person** is **in principle excluded** from that AVG protection (pt 24, cross-ref pt 27).
- **But** a functional address (`info@onderneming.com`, `dpo@onderneming.com`, …) **can be personal data** when, in the circumstances, it can be linked to a natural person — e.g. used and managed **exclusively by one person** in their role (pt 27).
- GBA cites **Geschillenkamer beslissing 40/2023** of 3 April 2023: <https://www.gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit.be/publications/beslissing-ten-gronde-nr.-40-2023.pdf>. That decision (verified): a functional mailbox that only one person used **was** that person’s personal data for that period; once several staff used it, it **stopped** being that person’s personal data.

**PDF:** <https://www.gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit.be/publications/aanbeveling-01-2025-over-de-verwerking-van-persoonsgegevens-bij-direct-marketing.pdf>  
(GBA notes the Dutch file was the public-consultation version; treat the French final as controlling if they diverge. **UNVERIFIED** whether a later Dutch final was published after this note.)

**Operational read (research, not a stamp):**

- Public `info@` on a club domain, used as the organisation contact, is the address the KB and FOD describe. Mail **that** box, not a named volunteer.
- If the public site says “mail Jan Peeters at info@…” and only Jan is listed, **GBA 40/2023** is in play. **UNVERIFIED** per club. Default: still only `info@`, never Jan’s Hotmail; if the page makes the inbox obviously one named human, **do not mail** without advice.
- AVG is **not** a licence to skip WER opt-out. The two statutes stack.

## 6. From-name pattern (house rule, grounded in XII.12 / XII.13, § 3)

The statute does **not** prescribe a display-name formula. **XII.12, 2°** requires the advertiser to be **identifiable**. **XII.13, § 3** forbids using a **third party’s identity** and **hiding the origin** of the message. FOD FAQ Q15’s example of meeting XII.12, 2° is a closing line *“Reclame verzonden door en voor rekening van XXXX”* plus a link to the sender’s particulars. FOD FAQ Q16 repeats the § 3 bans.

**House from-name pattern for first contact:**

```
From-name:  Voornaam Achternaam
From-addr:  voornaam@<your-own-domain>
```

Optional, still the same human: `Voornaam Achternaam | Club-site kit`.

**Do not send from:**

- personal **Hotmail / Outlook.com / Gmail / Yahoo / Skynet**;
- a display name that is not the real sender (`Support`, `Info`, `Team`, a made-up first name);
- a third-party domain or a spoofed club address (**XII.13, § 3, 1°–2°**).

**No personal Hotmail** applies to **both** ends: do not **from** a personal Hotmail, do not **to** a volunteer’s personal Hotmail.

**UNVERIFIED:** whether a particular transactional host (Sendgrid, etc.) that rewrites `Return-Path` counts as “hiding origin” under XII.13, § 3, 2°. If you use one, keep `From` as the real person on your domain and do not mask the brand as the club.

## 7. `u` for first contact (house rule, not in WER)

WER/KB/GBA do **not** regulate *u* vs *je*. **House rule:** first contact to a Belgian vzw board is **u** (and matching verb forms). Switch to *je* only if they do. This is Flemish B2B courtesy, not a statutory requirement. Marked so nobody files it as law.

## 8. Do not fight Twizzit (house rule, not in WER)

Twizzit is the membership / communication stack many Belgian clubs already pay for. This note is **not** a legal opinion on Twizzit.

**House rule:**

- Do **not** pitch a static kit, inbox pack, or pay page as a **Twizzit replacement**.
- Do **not** tell a club to leave Twizzit, migrate members off Twizzit, or fight Twizzit on comms.
- If the public site **is** Twizzit (or a current Twizzit/WordPress/Wix rebuild), **skip** — that club is not the thin-site `info@` buyer. See the separate buyer lists; this file does not re-list clubs.
- If a club uses Twizzit **and** still publishes a public `info@` on a thin brochure site, a first-contact offer is **brochure pages next to Twizzit**, not a war on Twizzit.

**UNVERIFIED:** any claim about Twizzit’s contract, data processing, or whether `@mail.twizzit.com` is a legal-person address. Do not mail Twizzit-hosted personal logins.

## 9. Checklist before anyone else mails (still not a stamp)

This file still **sends no mail**. If a later operator prepares a first contact, the research line is:

1. **Entity:** KBO Public Search shows a **vzw / ivzw / stichting** (legal person). No invented number. If absent → stop.
2. **To:** public **impersonal** mailbox on the **club domain** (`info@`, `contact@`, …), copied from a cited public page. **No personal Hotmail.** No `voornaam.naam@`.
3. **Offer:** aimed at the **organisation** (site / admin / invoicing for the vzw), not at members as consumers.
4. **Not Twizzit-as-primary.** Do not fight Twizzit.
5. **From:** real **Voornaam Achternaam** on **your own domain**. Never personal Hotmail. Never a third-party identity.
6. **Register:** **u**. Commercial nature obvious; identify the natural/legal person for whose account the mail is sent (**XII.12, 2°**).
7. **Opt-out in the same message**, electronic, free, no reason (**XII.13, § 2** + **KB art. 2**). Honour it; non-promotional ack within 24–48 h (FOD reading).
8. **One first contact**, not a drip. FOD FAQ Q8’s “ask consent by a first non-advertising mail” is a **different** path, for addresses that **need** opt-in. Do not mix it with a product pitch to `info@`.
9. Keep proof that the **exception** applied (screenshot of public `info@` + KBO legal form). XII.13, § 4 / FOD Q13.

If any row fails, **do not mail**.

## 10. UNVERIFIED (do not upgrade these to law)

- That a **named** club in another file is a filed vzw until KBO Public Search says so.
- That a given `info@` is not in practice one identifiable volunteer (**GBA 01/2025 pt 27 / 40/2023**).
- That the word **“reclame”** must appear in the subject of an obviously commercial B2B mail (**XII.12, 1°** is impression-based; FOD Q15 is case-by-case).
- That a purely informational “may we send a quote?” mail is outside *reclame* (**I.18, 6°** is broad; FOD Q6 calls the line delicate). Treat a product pitch as *reclame*.

- Whether ePrivacy Regulation talks replace XII.13. FOD FAQ 2019 already said the EU file was pending. **UNVERIFIED** as of 2026-08-27 that XII.13 + KB 2003 are still the Belgian pair — they were the pair in every official text retrieved today.
